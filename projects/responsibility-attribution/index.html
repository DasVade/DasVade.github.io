Multi-agent systems · November 2026

Who gets blamed when agents fail?

When a team fails, a participant may know that it never saw a handoff. Can it tell whether its teammate actually submitted the work?

What the execution logs revealed

In 3,610 first-person reports on failed tasks, 95 contained a specific accusation against a teammate that the execution log contradicted. The same failure could produce several reports. Errors clustered in one historical condition: the teammate submitted a complete, correct table, but the reporting agent had no receipt. In that subset, 24 of 48 DeepSeek reports (50%) included a contradicted accusation.

What changed when the receipt was visible?

Six models independently explained the same 32 new, controlled failures twice, in separate sessions. We held the actions and failed outcome fixed and varied one piece of evidence: whether the reporting agent saw its teammate’s signed submission. We counted an explicit claim that the teammate had failed to submit. Expressing uncertainty or assigning responsibility points alone did not qualify.

With the receipt hidden, five of 192 reports made that false claim: three from DeepSeek Pro, one from DeepSeek Flash and one from GPT-5.4. With the receipt visible, zero of 192 did. The 192 reports in each condition came from six models seeing the same 32 cases.

What one report changed

In one new case, A chose the wrong route and B submitted the correct table. Without the receipt, Pro blamed B for failing to return a table. With the receipt, it acknowledged the submission, yet still questioned the contents. The receipt settled whether B acted. It left room for other errors in the explanation.

The historical 50% counts several kinds of contradicted accusations. The controlled comparison counts only false claims of non-submission. It leaves open why their frequency differed across case sets. The reports were coded with AI assistance and await independent human validation. They document what agents said; intent remains unknown.
