Recover scene geometry from calibrated stereo, then evaluate a neural radiance field on independent views of a static scene.

NeRF: independent-view evaluation

24.19 dB PSNR / 0.831 SSIM averaged over six held-out 100×100 test views of one synthetic Lego scene. A separate validation set selected the checkpoint.

The fresh model uses 32 samples per ray and known camera poses. Camera-convention checks corrected the image-down ray sign; finite-interval rendering and analytic opacity tests make assumptions explicit. This is a course-method reproduction and experimental extension, not a new NeRF algorithm.

Training, splits and metric scope

90 training views (0–89), five validation views (90/92/94/96/98), six test views (100–105). Seed 5670; 20,000 training steps; 1,024 rays per step; RTX 4070 Ti; approximately 104 s including validation. The test renderer is fixed at 32 samples. Mean PSNR is averaged over per-view dB values. SSIM uses an 11×11 Gaussian window with sigma 1.5 and RGB averaging.

The test views are disjoint for this fresh model, but earlier legacy experiments inspected some images. This is a small development evaluation, not an externally untouched benchmark.

Calibrated stereo: images to colored geometry

Rectification aligns calibrated image pairs for disparity matching. SSD, SAD and ZNCC are compared at matched resolution and filtering settings; the resulting depth is back-projected and colored from the image.

A separate render-only sampling study

With the 32-sample-trained checkpoint frozen, validation PSNR increases from 24.08 dB at 32 render samples to 24.60 at 64 and 24.64 at 128. Mean time rises from 0.024 to 0.039 and 0.079 s/view. This changes inference quadrature only and does not establish an effect of training sample count.

Coursework and implementation contributions

Penn CIS 5800 supplied the tasks, data, calibration, architecture and frameworks. The consolidation preserves a traceable baseline. Extensions include camera/ray audits, the image-y sign correction, finite rendering checks, validation-selected training, explicit independent-view evaluation, reproducible exports and controlled experiments, developed with coding-assistant support. File presence does not establish authorship of every supplied line.

Limitations

One static synthetic NeRF scene, one training seed and 100×100 images limit generalization claims. Thin structures, specular surfaces and edges retain errors. Stereo has holes, outliers and scene-specific filtering; no depth-accuracy claim is supported. Known calibration is assumed. This is neither SLAM nor a dynamic world model, and no hardware deployment is demonstrated.
